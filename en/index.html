Trajectory

From technical autonomy to team leadership

The Event Project Management course served a broader goal than learning about the events industry. Its three blocks — contributing to strategy, designing the project and then managing delivery — matched the commercial, strategic and collective dimensions I wanted to add to my technical foundation.

  • My attendance certificate records 1,000 training hours. My actual investment was probably higher once personal work and deeper study are included; I nevertheless keep 1,000 hours as the only verifiable figure in professional documents.
  • In professional roles, I have mainly worked with a high degree of autonomy. As a technical referent, I explained decisions, reviewed code and supported developers, but I claim limited experience in line management or formal team leadership.
  • I transferred concepts from the course into Sonomundi: need and MVP framing, prioritization, specifications, planning, risk management, role allocation, readiness indicators, reporting, feedback collection and continuous improvement.
  • With Sonomundi, I now practise another form of coordination: explicit goals and specifications, work distributed across specialized agents, bounded contexts and responsibilities, outcome tracking, arbitration and final validation.
  • A team of agents is not a substitute for a human team. This practice nevertheless strengthens transferable skills in framing, delegation, feedback, quality control and decision-making that I want to bring to a collaborative environment.

My goal is to grow into a role where I can remain hands-on while helping a team understand priorities, make sound decisions and deliver together.
